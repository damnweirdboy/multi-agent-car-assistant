CATEGORY: maintenance
Oil change is one of the most important regular maintenance tasks. Conventional motor oil should be changed every 5,000–7,500 km or every 6 months, whichever comes first. Synthetic motor oil can last 10,000–15,000 km or up to 12 months. Always use the oil viscosity specified in your owner's manual (e.g., 5W-30, 0W-20). Along with the oil, always replace the oil filter at each oil change. Fresh oil keeps the engine lubricated, cool, and clean of metal particles and sludge.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Air filter replacement: The engine air filter should be inspected every 15,000 km and replaced every 20,000–30,000 km or once a year. A clogged air filter reduces engine power, fuel efficiency, and can cause rough idling. In dusty environments, replace more frequently. The cabin air filter (for the HVAC system) should be replaced every 15,000–20,000 km to maintain clean air inside the vehicle.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Tire rotation and inspection: Tires should be rotated every 10,000–12,000 km to ensure even wear across all four tires. Uneven wear shortens tire life and affects handling. Check tire pressure monthly — incorrect pressure reduces fuel efficiency and affects safety. The recommended tire pressure is printed on a sticker inside the driver's door jamb. Check tread depth with a tread depth gauge; replace tires when tread is below 1.6mm (legally) or 3mm (recommended for safety).

CATEGORY: maintenance
Brake inspection and service: Brake pads should be inspected every 20,000 km and typically last 30,000–70,000 km depending on driving style. Replace brake pads when thickness falls below 3mm. Brake rotors should be inspected at each pad change and replaced or resurfaced when worn. Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time and should be flushed every 2 years or 40,000 km. Use the brake fluid type specified in your owner's manual (DOT 3, DOT 4, or DOT 5.1).

CATEGORY: maintenance
Spark plug replacement: Standard copper spark plugs should be replaced every 30,000–45,000 km. Iridium or platinum spark plugs last 60,000–100,000 km. Worn spark plugs cause poor fuel economy, rough idling, difficulty starting, and misfires. Signs of failing spark plugs include the check engine light, engine stumbling, or noticeably reduced performance.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Coolant / antifreeze flush: The engine coolant should be flushed and replaced every 2 years or 40,000–50,000 km (for green coolant) or every 5 years / 150,000 km for long-life OAT/HOAT coolants. Old coolant becomes acidic and corrodes the cooling system components (radiator, water pump, hoses). Always use the coolant type and mixture ratio specified by the manufacturer.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Transmission fluid service: Automatic transmission fluid should be changed every 60,000–100,000 km or every 4 years. Manual transmission fluid should be replaced every 50,000–80,000 km. Using the correct fluid type is critical — using the wrong fluid can damage the transmission. Signs that fluid needs changing include dark/burnt-smelling fluid, delayed shifting, or slipping gears.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Battery maintenance: Car batteries typically last 3–5 years. Inspect battery terminals annually for corrosion (white/blue buildup) — clean with a mixture of baking soda and water. Ensure the battery hold-down is secure. In cold climates, battery capacity drops significantly — consider testing the battery before winter. A battery load test can determine if it still holds adequate charge.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Power steering fluid: Check power steering fluid level every 6 months. Low fluid causes heavy steering and whining noise when turning. Flush and replace power steering fluid every 50,000–80,000 km or if the fluid is dark and dirty. Many modern vehicles use electric power steering which requires no fluid maintenance.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Timing belt replacement: The timing belt is a critical component that keeps the engine's camshaft and crankshaft synchronized. It must be replaced at the manufacturer's specified interval — typically every 60,000–100,000 km or every 5–7 years. Failure to replace a worn timing belt can result in it snapping, which causes catastrophic engine damage (bent valves, damaged pistons). Many modern vehicles use a timing chain instead, which generally lasts the life of the engine.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Wiper blades: Replace windshield wiper blades every 12 months or when they start leaving streaks, skipping, or making noise. In climates with harsh winters, winter wiper blades are recommended from October to March. Keep the windshield washer fluid reservoir filled — use a proper washer fluid, not plain water, to prevent freezing and to effectively remove bugs and road grime.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Complete service schedule summary: Every 5,000–10,000 km — oil and filter change. Every 10,000–12,000 km — tire rotation, visual brake inspection. Every 20,000–30,000 km — air filter check, cabin air filter replacement. Every 30,000–50,000 km — coolant check, spark plug inspection. Every 60,000–100,000 km — timing belt, transmission fluid, brake fluid flush. Annual — battery test, wiper blades, tire pressure check, visual inspection of all fluids and hoses.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Replacing the cabin air filter is crucial for interior air quality. It should be replaced every 15,000 to 20,000 km. A clogged cabin filter dramatically reduces the airflow from the HVAC system, causes musty or mildew-like smells, and places immense strain on the blower motor, potentially causing it to burn out early. Changing it takes less than 10 minutes on most modern cars and requires no tools.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Fuel injector cleaning services are highly recommended every 50,000 km. Over time, carbon deposits and fuel varnish clog the microscopic nozzles of the fuel injectors, ruining the fuel spray pattern. Having a professional chemical cleaning restores the atomization of the fuel, which directly resolves rough idling issues, restores lost engine horsepower, and improves overall fuel economy.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Cleaning the throttle body every 50,000 km is a simple yet vital maintenance task. The throttle body controls the air entering the engine, and carbon buildup around the butterfly valve causes it to stick. This sticking leads to a very rough or fluctuating idle and a delayed, sluggish response when you press the gas pedal. A simple spray of specialized throttle body cleaner and a wipe-down completely resolves this.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Brake fluid flushing is arguably the most neglected maintenance item. Brake fluid is highly hygroscopic, meaning it constantly absorbs moisture from the atmosphere. Over two years, it absorbs enough water to significantly lower its boiling point, leading to brake fade under heavy braking, and causing internal rust and corrosion that destroys expensive ABS modules and master cylinders. It must be flushed every 24 months.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Power steering fluid flush is recommended every 80,000 km. As the fluid ages, it breaks down from extreme heat and becomes contaminated with microscopic metal shavings from the steering rack gears. Flushing out the dark, degraded fluid and replacing it with fresh fluid prevents the steering pump from whining and dramatically prolongs the life of the very expensive steering rack assembly.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Differential fluid replacement is absolutely critical for RWD, AWD, and 4WD vehicles. The differential gears experience extreme friction and heat. The heavy gear oil should generally be changed every 50,000 to 80,000 km. Neglecting this causes the fluid to break down, leading to severe gear wear, a loud whining noise from the rear of the car, and ultimately a catastrophic differential failure.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Transfer case fluid on 4WD and AWD vehicles is essential for engaging the front and rear axles. It should be changed strictly every 50,000 km. Because the transfer case holds a relatively small amount of fluid, it degrades quickly. Ignoring this service leads to a stretched transfer case chain, binding when turning, and can completely destroy the 4WD system, resulting in thousands of dollars in repairs.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Valve clearance adjustment (also known as a valve lash adjustment) is a required service on certain engines, notably many Honda and Subaru models, typically every 100,000 km. As the engine wears, the gap between the camshaft and the valves changes. If they become too loose, the engine ticks loudly; if too tight, the valves can burn up and cause a severe misfire and loss of compression.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Lubricating chassis components is essential if your vehicle's suspension features grease fittings (zerks). This applies primarily to older vehicles or heavy-duty trucks. The ball joints, tie rod ends, and U-joints should be pumped with fresh chassis grease during every single oil change to force out water and dirt. Sealed 'maintenance-free' joints cannot be greased and must simply be replaced when they fail.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Washing the undercarriage of your vehicle thoroughly, especially during and after the winter season in snowy climates, is vital for vehicle longevity. Road salt and brine used for melting ice are highly corrosive. Leaving them on the undercarriage rapidly accelerates rust formation, which will aggressively eat through the structural frame, brake lines, and the entire exhaust system.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Battery terminal cleaning is a fast, crucial maintenance step. Lead-acid batteries naturally vent corrosive gases that cause fluffy white or blue acidic buildup on the terminals. This corrosion acts as an insulator, preventing the alternator from fully charging the battery and preventing the starter from receiving full power. Cleaning them with a wire brush and baking soda ensures a reliable start every time.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Headlight restoration is an important safety maintenance task. Over several years of UV exposure, the factory clear coat on plastic headlight lenses oxidizes, turning them cloudy and yellow. This severely diffuses the light beam, dramatically reducing your nighttime visibility. Using a restoration kit to wet-sand and polish the lenses, followed by applying a new UV protectant clear coat, restores factory-level illumination.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Windshield treatment with hydrophobic rain-repellent solutions (like Rain-X) is an excellent preventative maintenance habit. By applying this coating to the exterior glass every few months, rainwater aggressively beads up and rolls off the windshield at driving speeds, drastically improving visibility during torrential downpours. It also makes it significantly easier to scrape off winter ice and clean off dead bugs.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Lubricating door hinges and hood latches is a commonly forgotten task. Spraying white lithium grease on the door hinges and the hood/trunk latch mechanisms twice a year prevents annoying squeaks and groans. More importantly, it prevents the metal mechanisms from binding and seizing up over time, ensuring your doors always close securely and your hood doesn't get stuck shut.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Applying silicone spray to rubber weatherstripping around the doors and trunk keeps the rubber soft, pliable, and properly sealed against wind noise and water leaks. Crucially, in freezing winter climates, a coating of silicone spray prevents the wet rubber seals from freezing solidly to the metal door frames, meaning you won't tear the weatherstripping trying to yank your frozen car doors open.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Clearing sunroof drains is a vital annual maintenance task for any vehicle equipped with a sunroof. Sunroof assemblies are not completely waterproof; water that slips past the glass seal is caught in a tray and routed down four small rubber drain tubes hidden in the car's pillars. If these tubes clog with leaves or dirt, water overflows directly into the cabin, ruining the headliner and flooding the floorboards.

CATEGORY: maintenance
AC system service and inspection should be performed every 3 to 5 years. Even in a perfectly sealed air conditioning system, a tiny amount of refrigerant naturally permeates through the rubber hoses over time. Having a shop hook up an AC machine to evacuate the old refrigerant, check for vacuum leaks, and recharge it with the exact factory weight of freon ensures the system blows ice-cold during peak summer heat.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Wheel alignment should be proactively checked at least once a year, or immediately after hitting a severe pothole or curb. Even minor driving impacts knock the suspension geometry out of factory specifications. Driving with bad alignment causes the car to drift to one side, creates a crooked steering wheel, and will completely destroy a brand new set of tires with uneven edge wear in just a few thousand miles.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Tire balancing is strictly necessary whenever new tires are installed, but should also be re-checked if you feel a vibration in the steering wheel or seat at highway speeds. A tire balance uses small lead weights attached to the rim to ensure the heavy spots of the tire and wheel assembly are perfectly counter-weighted, allowing the wheel to spin flawlessly smooth without bouncing or wobbling.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Inspecting and replacing the PCV (Positive Crankcase Ventilation) valve every 50,000 km is incredibly cheap insurance for your engine. This $10 plastic valve allows harmful combustion blow-by gases to escape the crankcase. If it clogs, internal engine pressure builds rapidly, forcing oil to blow past the main engine seals, causing massive oil leaks and internal sludge buildup.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Cleaning the MAF (Mass Air Flow) sensor with a specialized electronics cleaner is a great habit every time you replace the engine air filter. The tiny heated wires inside the MAF sensor become coated in microscopic dust and oil vapor over time, causing it to misread the incoming air volume. A clean MAF sensor guarantees crisp throttle response and optimal fuel injection mapping.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Replacing wiper blades strictly every 6 to 12 months is a vital safety requirement. Rubber wiper blades rapidly degrade from UV sunlight and extreme temperature changes, causing them to harden, crack, and leave dangerous streaks or completely skip over patches of the windshield. Do not forget to inspect and replace the rear window wiper blade on SUVs and hatchbacks as well.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Inspecting CV (Constant Velocity) boots is a critical part of every under-car inspection. These rubber accordion-like boots on the front axles protect the complex CV joints. If the rubber dry-rots and tears, it flings all the lubricating grease out and allows abrasive road dirt and water in. Catching a torn boot early means a $50 boot replacement; ignoring it guarantees a $500 full axle replacement.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Checking drive belt (serpentine belt) tension and condition is required every 50,000 km. Look closely for deep cracks, fraying edges, or missing chunks of rubber on the ribbed side of the belt. A loose or worn belt will slip on the pulleys, causing a loud squeal and poor performance of the alternator and AC compressor. Also, inspect the automatic spring tensioner to ensure it holds the belt tight.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Flushing the heater core is a highly specific maintenance task required when your cabin heater starts blowing lukewarm or cold air despite the engine being fully warmed up. Over years, neglecting to change the engine coolant allows rust and mineral scale to completely plug the tiny aluminum tubes inside the heater core. A pressurized back-flush can often dislodge the blockage and restore winter heat.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Replacing the fuel filter (on vehicles equipped with an external, inline filter) every 50,000 km ensures that only perfectly clean fuel reaches the delicate fuel injectors. A heavily clogged fuel filter restricts fuel pressure under heavy acceleration, causing the engine to hesitate and stumble, and forces the electric fuel pump in the gas tank to work twice as hard, leading to its premature failure.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Inspecting rubber exhaust hangers is a simple visual check. The entire heavy metal exhaust system is suspended under the car by thick rubber mounts that isolate vibration. Over time, these rubber hangers stretch, dry-rot, and snap. If an exhaust pipe sags, it places massive stress on the engine manifold bolts and catalytic converter flanges, eventually causing the metal exhaust pipes to fracture completely.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Checking the spare tire air pressure is the single most forgotten maintenance task. Spare tires sitting in the trunk or mounted under the truck bed naturally lose air pressure over months and years. Checking it twice a year and filling it to the recommended 60 PSI (for compact spares) ensures that when you actually suffer a flat tire on the highway, your backup tire is safe and ready to drive on.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Cleaning battery cable ground connections resolves a massive percentage of modern automotive electrical gremlins. The thick negative battery cable bolts directly to the painted metal chassis and the engine block. Over time, rust and corrosion build up between the bolt and the frame, creating electrical resistance. Unbolting the ground wire, sanding the metal surface bare, and reattaching it ensures perfect electrical flow.

CATEGORY: maintenance
Replacing rubber engine mounts is a significant maintenance milestone usually reached around 150,000 km. These thick rubber blocks hold the heavy engine securely to the frame. As the rubber degrades, tears, or leaks its hydraulic fluid, the engine shifts violently under acceleration. Replacing worn mounts completely transforms the feel of the car, restoring a buttery-smooth idle and crisp transmission shifting feel.
