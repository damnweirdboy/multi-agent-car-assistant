Engine overheating is a serious problem that must be addressed immediately. Common causes include: low coolant level (check and refill with the correct coolant mixture), a faulty thermostat (stuck closed, preventing coolant flow), a blocked or leaking radiator, a broken water pump, a blown head gasket (look for white smoke from exhaust), or a failing cooling fan. If the temperature gauge is in the red zone, pull over safely, turn off the engine, and do not open the radiator cap while the engine is hot. Allow the engine to cool for at least 30 minutes before inspecting.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Low coolant / antifreeze is a common cause of engine overheating. The coolant reservoir is a translucent plastic tank near the radiator. Check the MIN and MAX markings. If the level is below MIN, add a 50/50 mixture of distilled water and antifreeze. A coolant level that keeps dropping indicates a leak — inspect hoses, the radiator, and the water pump for cracks or wet spots.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Radiator problems can cause overheating and cooling system failure. Signs of radiator issues include: coolant leaking under the car (usually green, orange, or pink liquid), a sweet smell from the engine bay, visible rust or corrosion on the radiator fins, or the engine reaching operating temperature too slowly or too quickly. A blocked radiator can often be flushed; a physically damaged one must be replaced.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Strange knocking or ticking noise from the engine can indicate several issues: low engine oil level (check dipstick immediately), worn piston rings, a loose or failing timing chain, or exhaust manifold leaks. A knocking sound that worsens under acceleration often points to rod bearing failure — this is serious and requires immediate attention. A ticking sound on startup that fades away is usually caused by hydraulic lifters and is less critical but should be checked.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Brake problems are safety-critical and should never be ignored. Squealing brakes usually indicate worn brake pads — the wear indicator is making contact with the rotor. Grinding brakes mean the pads are completely worn through, causing metal-on-metal contact, which damages the rotors. A pulsating brake pedal suggests warped rotors. A soft or spongy pedal indicates air in the brake lines or a failing master cylinder. If the brake warning light is on, check the brake fluid level first.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Check Engine Light (CEL) or Malfunction Indicator Lamp (MIL) illuminated means the car's OBD-II system has detected a fault code. Common causes include: loose or faulty gas cap (causing evaporative emissions code P0442), failing oxygen sensor (P0136, P0141), catalytic converter efficiency below threshold (P0420), mass air flow sensor fault (P0100), or misfires (P0300–P0304). A blinking check engine light indicates an active misfire that can damage the catalytic converter — reduce speed and seek service immediately.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Transmission problems manifest as: difficulty shifting gears, slipping between gears (engine revs but car does not accelerate), delayed engagement when shifting from Park to Drive, unusual sounds during gear changes, or a burning smell. Low transmission fluid is the most common cause — check the level and condition (should be red/pink, not dark brown or burnt-smelling). Transmission fluid should be changed every 60,000–100,000 km.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Car vibration while driving can come from multiple sources. Vibration at highway speeds is often caused by unbalanced or damaged wheels/tires. Vibration during braking points to warped brake rotors. Vibration during acceleration may indicate worn CV joints or axle shaft issues. Vibration in the steering wheel at low speeds often means worn tie rod ends or ball joints. Identifying when the vibration occurs (speed, braking, turning) helps narrow down the cause.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Battery and electrical problems include: difficulty starting the engine (slow cranking), the battery warning light on the dashboard, dim headlights, or the car not starting at all. A car battery typically lasts 3–5 years. Test the battery voltage — a fully charged battery should read 12.6V; below 12.0V indicates a discharged or failing battery. Check the alternator output (should be 13.5–14.5V when running) to ensure it is charging the battery.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Oil pressure warning light (low oil pressure) is a critical warning — stop the engine as soon as it is safe to do so. Causes include: critically low oil level, a failing oil pump, blocked oil passages, or a faulty oil pressure sensor. Running the engine with low oil pressure can cause catastrophic engine damage within minutes. Check the oil level with the dipstick after the engine has been off for a few minutes.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A bad alternator typically manifests as a battery-shaped warning light on the dashboard. Because it fails to charge the battery while the engine runs, you will experience dimming headlights, slow power windows, and eventually, the engine will stall completely while driving. A healthy alternator should output between 13.5 and 14.5 volts when the engine is running. Ignoring a failing alternator will leave you stranded and completely drain and potentially ruin the car's battery.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing starter motor will often produce a single loud 'click' or rapid clicking sound when you turn the key, but the engine won't crank. This can be differentiated from a dead battery by testing the battery voltage; if the battery is fully charged (12.6V) but the car still only clicks, the starter is likely the culprit. Sometimes, tapping the starter lightly with a hammer can temporarily engage it, confirming the diagnosis.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing fuel pump often causes sputtering at high speeds, a sudden loss of power under stress (like driving uphill), or a complete 'crank but no start' condition. You can sometimes diagnose this by listening carefully near the gas tank when turning the key to the 'ON' position; you should hear a faint humming sound as the pump primes. If there is no sound, the pump or its relay has likely failed.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Symptoms of a bad Mass Air Flow (MAF) sensor include rough idling, frequent stalling, extremely poor fuel economy, and intense hesitation during acceleration. The MAF sensor measures the amount of air entering the engine, and when dirty or faulty, it throws off the air-fuel ratio. This almost always triggers a Check Engine Light, commonly with a P0101 OBD-II code. Cleaning it with a specialized MAF cleaner is the first step before replacement.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A faulty oxygen (O2) sensor is one of the most common causes of a Check Engine Light. When it fails, the engine computer cannot correctly adjust the air-fuel mixture, causing the car to run 'rich' (too much fuel). This leads to drastically reduced fuel efficiency, a prominent rotten egg smell from the exhaust (sulfur), and rough engine idling. Prompt replacement prevents long-term damage to the catalytic converter.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Ignition coil failure leads directly to engine misfires. When a coil fails, the spark plug doesn't fire, causing the engine to stumble, shake heavily at idle, and lose significant power during acceleration. A misfire will typically cause the Check Engine Light to flash continuously, which is a warning that unburned fuel is entering the exhaust and melting the catalytic converter. Stop driving immediately to prevent expensive secondary damage.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A clogged catalytic converter severely restricts exhaust gas flow, causing a massive drop in engine power. The vehicle will feel incredibly sluggish, may refuse to accelerate past a certain speed, and you might notice intense heat radiating from the floorboards. In extreme cases, the converter can become red hot and smell strongly of sulfur. This usually requires a highly expensive replacement.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Vacuum leaks occur when unmetered air enters the intake manifold past the MAF sensor, causing the engine to run 'lean'. Symptoms include an unusually high or wildly fluctuating idle, a distinct hissing sound from the engine bay, and stalling when coming to a stop. Smoke testing the intake system is the most effective way to pinpoint cracked vacuum hoses or failing intake gaskets causing the leak.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A faulty Throttle Position Sensor (TPS) feeds incorrect data about the accelerator pedal's position to the engine computer. This can cause unpredictable and erratic idle speeds, sudden stalling without warning, or intense hesitation when you press the gas pedal. The car may jerk violently during acceleration as the transmission struggles to determine the correct gear, making the vehicle dangerous to drive in heavy traffic.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Symptoms of a failing water pump include visible puddles of green or orange coolant under the front of the engine, chronic overheating, and a distinct whining or grinding noise coming from the water pump pulley due to a failing internal bearing. If the water pump is driven by the timing belt, it is critical to replace both at the same time to prevent catastrophic engine failure if the pump seizes.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A thermostat regulates the engine's operating temperature. If it fails stuck-open, the engine will run far too cool, leading to abysmal fuel economy and a heater that blows lukewarm air in the winter. If it fails stuck-closed, the engine will rapidly overheat because hot coolant cannot flow to the radiator. A stuck-closed thermostat requires immediate replacement before the head gasket blows.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Worn wheel bearings produce a distinct humming, rumbling, or growling noise that changes pitch with vehicle speed. The noise often gets significantly louder when turning slightly in one direction and quieter in the other, which helps identify which side of the car has the failing bearing. Delaying repairs can cause the wheel to completely detach from the vehicle while driving.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing CV joint (Constant Velocity joint) makes a very distinct, rhythmic clicking or popping noise. This noise is most prominent when accelerating through a sharp turn, such as navigating a parking lot or a tight intersection. It is almost always caused by a torn rubber CV boot that allowed grease to escape and abrasive dirt to enter, destroying the joint's bearings.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Worn tie rod ends cause a loose, sloppy feeling in the steering wheel and cause the vehicle to wander randomly within its lane. You may also notice severely uneven tire wear on the inner or outer edges, and hear a clunking noise from the front suspension when driving over small bumps. Replacing them requires a professional wheel alignment immediately afterward.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Failing shock absorbers or struts drastically degrade ride quality and safety. The most obvious symptom is excessive bouncing—the car continues to bounce several times after hitting a bump instead of settling immediately. You will also experience poor handling in corners, a 'nose-dive' feeling when braking hard, significantly longer stopping distances, and cupped or scalloped tire wear patterns.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing brake master cylinder is incredibly dangerous. The primary symptom is a soft, spongy brake pedal that slowly sinks all the way to the floorboard when you hold steady pressure on it at a stoplight. This indicates that the internal rubber seals have completely failed and brake fluid is bypassing the piston, drastically reducing your ability to stop the vehicle safely.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A stuck or seized brake caliper will cause the car to pull violently to one side during hard braking. You will also notice extremely uneven brake pad wear, where one pad is ground down to the metal while the others look new. The affected wheel will radiate excessive heat and may smell like burning friction material after a short drive. The caliper must be rebuilt or replaced.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Worn or broken engine mounts fail to isolate the engine's vibrations from the cabin. You will feel excessive vibration through the steering wheel and floorboards, especially while idling in gear. More severely, you will hear a loud clunking or banging noise during hard acceleration or when shifting from reverse to drive, as the entire engine physically shifts out of place under the hood.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing transmission torque converter can cause a pronounced shuddering feeling at highway speeds, almost as if you are driving over rumble strips. It can also cause slipping, where the engine RPMs spike but the vehicle doesn't accelerate, or a complete failure to engage overdrive gears. These issues are often accompanied by an illuminated Check Engine Light and transmission fault codes.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Low automatic transmission fluid is the leading cause of transmission issues. Symptoms include delayed gear engagement (especially when shifting into Drive on cold mornings), slipping between gears, incredibly harsh or jerky shifts, and an overall sluggish feel. If the fluid smells burnt or looks dark brown instead of a healthy translucent pink, the transmission has already suffered internal wear.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A stuck Positive Crankcase Ventilation (PCV) valve traps pressure inside the engine block. This excessive crankcase pressure will literally blow out the engine's oil seals, causing severe oil leaks. It also causes a rough idle and forces oil vapor to be sucked into the intake manifold, carbon-fouling the spark plugs and creating thick blue exhaust smoke.

CATEGORY: diagnose
The Exhaust Gas Recirculation (EGR) valve reduces emissions. If it gets clogged with carbon and sticks open, it causes a severely rough idle, stalling at stoplights, and poor acceleration. If it sticks completely closed, it raises combustion chamber temperatures, which causes audible engine pinging (detonation or knocking) under heavy load and drastically increases harmful NOx emissions.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing power steering pump will emit a loud, groaning or whining noise that directly correlates with engine RPM and gets significantly louder when you turn the steering wheel. The steering will also feel incredibly heavy and difficult to turn, especially at low parking lot speeds. Checking the fluid reservoir for low levels or aerated, bubbly fluid is the first diagnostic step.

CATEGORY: diagnose
An AC compressor clutch failure means the air conditioning system simply will not turn on. The most obvious symptom is that the HVAC vents blow completely warm ambient air despite the AC button being illuminated. You can visually inspect the front of the AC compressor while the engine is running; if the outer clutch plate is stationary while the belt spins, the clutch or the electrical relay has failed.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A blown head gasket is a catastrophic failure that allows coolant to mix with the engine oil or enter the combustion chamber. Symptoms include milky, chocolate-milk-colored engine oil, thick, sweet-smelling white smoke billowing from the exhaust, completely unexplained coolant loss without external puddles, and rapid, uncontrollable engine overheating.

CATEGORY: diagnose
Worn internal piston rings fail to seal the combustion chamber. This causes the engine to burn massive amounts of oil, resulting in a constant plume of blue-tinted exhaust smoke, especially under heavy acceleration. You will experience a severe loss of engine power, incredibly poor cylinder compression, and the spark plugs will constantly become fouled with thick, black, oily carbon.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A failing ignition switch can cause bizarre electrical gremlins. The car may die suddenly and completely while driving without any warning, or it may fail to crank entirely when you turn the key. This is often accompanied by flickering dashboard lights, the radio cutting in and out, or accessories failing to power on when the key is turned to the 'accessory' position.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A clogged fuel filter severely restricts fuel flow to the engine. The car will experience intense hesitation under heavy load (like accelerating onto a highway), poor overall acceleration, and frequent stalling. You may also hear a high-pitched whine from the rear of the car as the fuel pump works twice as hard trying to push fuel through the severe blockage.

CATEGORY: diagnose
An EVAP (Evaporative Emission Control) system leak is typically caused by a loose, missing, or degraded gas cap, or a cracked charcoal canister. It triggers a Check Engine Light (often code P0442 or P0456). While it rarely causes noticeable drivability issues, you may notice a faint smell of raw gasoline lingering around the rear of the vehicle, and the car will fail a state emissions inspection.

CATEGORY: diagnose
A faulty blower motor resistor dictates the speed of the interior HVAC fan. When it fails, the fan will completely stop working on the lower speed settings (1, 2, and 3), but will still work flawlessly on the absolute highest speed setting (4). This happens because the highest speed setting bypasses the blown resistor coils entirely, sending full voltage directly to the motor.
